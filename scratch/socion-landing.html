Fig. 02 — Method

People rarely describe their strongest side honestly

The block someone breathes through is the one they undersell, because it feels obvious to them. What they perform, defend, and over-explain out loud is usually the block they're weakest in. Standard tests ask what you'd like to be true. This reads for that gap instead.

01

Six scenario questions, no multiple choice

Each one asks you to narrate a real situation — a disagreement, a plan that changed, a room going flat — in full sentences. There's no menu of answers to pick the flattering one from.

02

The engine scores register, not content

For each of the eight elements it checks whether your language is fluid and self-generated, or borrowed, rule-quoting, and never quite satisfied — the same test Aušra used to separate the two in her own 1983 writing on logic and ethics.

03

You get the full Model A, not just a four-letter code

Which two functions you lead with, which two sit as quiet obligation, which two you'd rather someone else supplied, and which two you use without ever noticing you're using them.
